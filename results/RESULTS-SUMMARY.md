## C. Does series or proceedings metadata contaminate topic searches on this subject? (`prevalence_summary.json`, `prevalence_hand_label_summary.json`, `series_other_databases.csv`)
- **Scopus export (the actual case):** 15 of 168 records (8.9 percent) from one series; 13 off-topic (7.7 percent). The Phase-2 agent also finds 35 of 168 (20.8 percent) records that match only through keywords rather than title or abstract (`../bibliometrics/database-comparison.md`, flag R0).
- **Mechanism in other databases:** the 13 contaminated records are chapter-specific in Crossref and OpenAlex (OpenAlex keywords are the chapter's own; Crossref carries no subject), so none carries the volume-level keyword block; Phase 2 finds 0 of the 13 in its OpenAlex corpus. Tested on 15 DOIs by single-record lookups.
- **Crossref bibliographic search, 50 topic queries on green finance, fintech, ESG, climate finance, carbon neutrality, digital finance, top 100 each (5,000 rows, 3,899 unique DOIs):** 1,860 rows (37.2 percent) are book chapters, books or proceedings papers; in 653 rows the query terms appear only through the container title (361 of those rows in book or proceedings records, 301 unique records). Front-matter style titles (Index, Copyright, Foreword, Front Matter, Chapter 1 Introduction, or title equal to container) appear in 93 rows (73 unique). The "Global Fintech Institute - World Scientific Series on Fintech" series returns "Python Programming Basics", "Hypothesis Testing" and "Portfolio Analysis" for the query "fintech".
- **Hand check of 60 random container-only book/proceedings records:** 27 off-topic (45.0 percent; 95 percent CI 32 to 58: front/back matter 8, generic or unrelated chapters 19), 7 borderline, 26 relevant. Scaling to the 301 unique records gives roughly 135 contaminants, about 3.5 percent of the 3,899 unique Crossref results (wide interval).
- **Semantic Scholar relevance search (title and abstract), first 7 of the 50 queries only (the rest were rate limited to zero results; 700 rows, 549 unique):** 2.4 percent non-journal; 1.6 percent of records with abstracts lack the query terms; no front matter. So the contamination depends on whether the engine matches container or keyword metadata.
- **OpenAlex candidate pool (Phase-2 data, read only):** of 3,241 fetched works, 24.3 percent came from book series, ebook platforms or conferences; after the title-and-abstract string test, 49 of 611 matches (8.0 percent) remain from those source types.
- Reading: the Scopus case is real (7.7 percent of the corpus, much larger in specific tables) but it was driven by Scopus author-keyword handling at volume level; in open databases the same kind of problem takes a different form (container-title matches and front matter), is larger in raw share (about 37 percent book-like rows in Crossref) but is mostly removable by a title-and-abstract test. The prevalence number is a property of these 50 queries and these three databases on 17 Oct 2026, not of the field.
